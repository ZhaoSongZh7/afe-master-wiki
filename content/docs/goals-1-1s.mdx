How goals work and how to make the most of your 1:1s.

## Setting goals

Your intern project plan doubles as your goal framework. On Day 1, your manager will walk you through your **Embark plan** which includes:

- **Project deliverables** broken into milestones with clear timelines
- **Technical learning goals** tied to your role (e.g., first CR by week 2, design doc by week 3)
- **Leadership Principles** to demonstrate — for AFE, the most important is **Learn and Be Curious**

### Tips for goal-setting

- Break big deliverables into weekly sub-goals with your manager
- Make each goal specific and measurable ("Submit my first CR" not "Get better at coding")
- Revisit goals at midpoint — scope up or down based on progress
- Track progress visibly (shared doc, Embark updates) so nothing is a surprise at evaluation
- **Schedule 1:1s strategically**: Consider booking your manager 1:1s for Monday mornings. This helps you start the week with clear priorities instead of scrambling mid-week.

## Running good 1:1s

### Cadence

- **Weekly** with your manager (30 min minimum)
- **Daily or near-daily** check-ins with your tech mentor (Slack, quick standup, or ad-hoc)
- **Bi-weekly** with your career mentor (optional but recommended)

### How to prepare

Before each 1:1 with your manager, come with:

1. **Progress update** — what you accomplished since last week
2. **Blockers** — what's slowing you down (be specific about what you've already tried)
3. **Questions** — decisions you need input on, things you're unsure about
4. **Feedback request** — "Is there anything I should be doing differently?"

### Making 1:1s productive

- Own the agenda — this is **your** time, not a status report for your manager
- Share context on *how* you're doing, not just *what* you're doing
- Ask for specific examples when feedback is vague ("Can you give me an example of what 'more ownership' looks like?")
- Take notes and follow up on action items

## Feedback

### Asking for feedback

Don't wait for midpoint or final evaluations. Ask regularly:

- After code reviews: "What could I have done better?"
- After design discussions: "Was my approach reasonable?"
- In 1:1s: "On a scale of 1–5, where am I on [specific skill]? What would move me up?"

### Acting on feedback

- **Acknowledge** — thank the person and confirm you understand
- **Prioritize** — pick 1–2 things to improve this week, not everything at once
- **Show progress** — circle back next 1:1 with "You mentioned X, here's what I changed"
- **Ask for re-evaluation** — "Has my [area] improved since we talked?"

### Short feedback cycles matter

AFE interns benefit most from **frequent, specific** feedback with examples of "what good looks like." Incorporate CRs as early as possible after your design phase — the more iterations you get, the faster you grow.
