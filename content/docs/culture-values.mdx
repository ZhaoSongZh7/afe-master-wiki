How we work together and what we expect of each other.

## Amazon Leadership Principles

Amazon's culture is built on [16 Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/principles) (LPs). You'll hear them referenced in meetings, evaluations, and everyday decisions. Here are the ones most relevant to your AFE internship:

| Principle | What it means for you as an intern |
| --- | --- |
| **Learn and Be Curious** | The #1 LP for AFE interns. Ask questions, explore new tools, seek feedback. You're not expected to know everything — you're expected to actively learn. |
| **Ownership** | Take responsibility for your project deliverables. If something is unclear, ask. If something is blocked, raise it. |
| **Bias for Action** | Don't wait for perfect information. Try things, iterate, and learn from mistakes. |
| **Deliver Results** | Focus on shipping your project milestones. Quality matters, but done is better than perfect when time is limited. |
| **Earn Trust** | Be honest about progress and blockers. Ask for help early. Follow through on commitments. |
| **Dive Deep** | Understand *why* things work, not just *how*. Read the code, trace the call chain, understand the design. |

<Callout title="It's Always Day 1">
  This is Amazon's founding philosophy — maintain the urgency, curiosity, and customer focus of a startup, regardless of scale. As an intern, this means: stay curious, don't be afraid to question assumptions, and focus on impact.
</Callout>

## AFE-specific norms

### Expectations for AFE interns

- **No prior industry experience is expected.** You're here to learn. Ask questions freely.
- **You solve straightforward problems with support.** Your project is well-scoped by your manager; you're not expected to design complex architectures from scratch.
- **Ownership with guidelines.** You own your deliverables, but your manager and tech mentor set clear milestones and provide regular feedback.
- **Attempt to self-unblock first.** Use self-service resources (Sage, documentation, Slack) before asking for help — but don't spin for hours. A good rule: try for 30 minutes, then ask.
- **Short feedback cycles.** Submit CRs early and often. Getting feedback in week 3 is better than waiting until week 8.

### Communication norms

- **Slack** is the primary communication tool. Respond within a reasonable timeframe during work hours.
- **Ask your team** about their preferred communication cadence and channels on Day 1.
- **Be direct and concise** in written communication. Amazon values clear, structured writing.
- **Use 1:1s effectively** — bring an agenda, share blockers proactively, ask for feedback.

### Work-life balance

- Standard intern work hours align with your team's schedule (typically 9–5 or 10–6 local time).
- You are **not expected to work evenings or weekends.** If your project scope feels unmanageable within normal hours, talk to your manager about descoping.
- Take breaks, use your PTO, and lean on your support network.

## Code of conduct

Amazon employees (including interns) are expected to:

- Treat others with respect and professionalism
- Protect confidential information and intellectual property
- Follow Amazon's data security and privacy policies
- Report concerns about conduct, safety, or ethics

### Reporting a concern

- **Manager or skip-level** — for project or team concerns
- **HR / MyHR** ([atoz.amazon.work/myhr/livehelp/landing](https://atoz.amazon.work/myhr/livehelp/landing)) — for workplace concerns, harassment, or discrimination
- **AFE Program Team** ([afe-internship-program@amazon.com](mailto:afe-internship-program@amazon.com)) — for program-specific concerns
- **Ethics Hotline** — for anonymous reporting of serious concerns
- **tbh** — for mental health support (US-based interns)

</Callout>
